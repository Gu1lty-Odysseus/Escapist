# The-Reflection-Game
Echoes and Reflections have an escape.

"Every choice leaves an echo. Every lie creates a reflection."

No one remembers how they arrived.

Eight strangers awaken in a forgotten facility known only as *The Reflection Chamber*. Their memories of the last 24 hours have vanished, and the only thing connecting them is a small metal token engraved with a broken mirror.

A distorted voice fills the room.

> "You believe your reflection shows who you are. It doesn't. It only shows who you pretend to be."

The voice explains that every participant has made choices in their lives that affected others—acts of selfishness, cowardice, betrayal, or indifference. Some were intentional. Others were not. Today, they will face the echoes of those decisions.

The facility is divided into a series of chambers. Each room presents a dilemma with no perfect answer.

* Save time for the group by sacrificing something valuable.
* Tell the truth and lose an advantage, or lie and let someone else suffer the consequence.
* Choose who receives a limited resource.
* Decide whether to trust someone who has every reason to betray you.
* Determine whether justice and mercy can exist at the same time.

Every decision changes the path ahead. Doors unlock for some while closing for others. Information is never distributed equally. Every player sees only part of the truth.

Throughout the game, they discover recordings from previous groups. Some reveal that cooperation led to survival. Others claim that trust was their greatest mistake.

As the challenges continue, players begin noticing strange inconsistencies. Security footage shows people who look exactly like them making different decisions. Journals describe events they don't remember. Reflections in mirrors move moments before they do.

Eventually they uncover the truth.

There were never previous groups.

Every recording, every reflection, every warning came from alternate versions of themselves—people who made different choices within the same experiment.

The Reflection Chamber was never designed to test intelligence.

It was built to answer one question:

*When every choice has a cost, who are you when no answer is truly right?*

In the final chamber, the players discover there is no "correct" ending. The exit opens only when the group reaches a unanimous decision about the fate of someone they have never met, knowing that whichever option they choose will permanently shape another version of themselves.

As they leave, the final recording plays:

> "The mirrors never judged you."

> "They only showed the person your choices created."

> "The echo begins now."
